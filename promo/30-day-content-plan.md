## Week 2: Professionalism (soft intro to the kit)

**Day 8**
A freelancer with a clear proposal wins more than one with a lower price. A good proposal: restates the problem, lists exact deliverables, shows a timeline, gives a price, and expires in 14 days.

**Day 9**
5 things every freelance contract needs: scope of work, deposit, number of revisions, ownership of the work after payment, and a termination clause. Missing any one of them can cost you real money.

**Day 10**
Your first client email sets the tone for the whole job. A kick-off message should say: what you need from them, when they'll get the first draft, how many revisions they have, and how to reach you.

**Day 11**
Invoice tip: send it the day the work is delivered, not "when you get around to it". Include a due date and a late-fee line. Faster invoices are paid faster.

**Day 12, kit mention**
I put my proposal, contract, onboarding checklist and invoice templates into one pack for freelancers. QAR 50, instant download: [LINK]

**Day 13**
Scope creep is not your client's fault. It's what happens when scope is not written down. One sentence in your proposal ("Not included: …") prevents most of it.

**Day 14, engagement post**
What's the worst client mistake that taught you to always use a contract? Share yours, and I'll share the clause that would have stopped it.
